The Reality

CHINA IS NOT BUILT FOR FOREIGN VISITORS

You've seen the TikTok videos. Chongqing looks incredible. But here's what those videos don't show you—the everyday struggles that turn a dream trip into a headache.

🗣️

Language Barrier

Nobody speaks English. Not the taxi drivers. Not the waiters. Not even at the hotel front desk.

I translate everything—menus, signs, conversations. You'll actually understand what you're eating and where you're going.

💳

Payment Nightmare

Cash? Almost useless. Credit cards? Rarely accepted. Your Visa from home? Forget it.

China runs on WeChat Pay and Alipay. I handle all payments and you just pay me one flat fee at the end.

🚕

Transportation Chaos

Didi (China's Uber) requires a Chinese phone number. Metro signs? All in Chinese. Taxi drivers don't use meters for foreigners.

I handle all transport. Didi, metro, or my private car. You focus on the experience, not the logistics.

🌶️

Food Roulette

Menus without pictures. No English. "Chicken" could mean anything. Spice levels that melt faces.

I order what you'll actually enjoy. Mild, medium, or face-melting—your call. And I know the clean, safe spots.

🏨

Accommodation Confusion

Not all hotels accept foreigners. Booking.com shows rooms that don't exist. Best location? Good luck figuring that out.

I recommend foreigner-friendly hotels in the right neighborhoods. Pre-check locations so you don't end up in an industrial zone.

📱

Internet Blackout

Google? Blocked. Instagram? Blocked. WhatsApp? Blocked. Your entire digital life disappears at the airport.

I set you up before you even land. VPN recommendations, eSIM options, and offline maps so you're never stranded.

🎭

Cultural Landmines

Is it rude to finish your food? Should you haggle? Why is everyone staring? Are you offending someone?

I explain cultural context in real time. No awkward moments, no accidental insults, just smooth interactions.

🛂

Visa & Entry Stress

144-hour transit? Tourist visa? What documents? What if immigration asks questions you can't answer?

Pre-arrival guidance on visa options, required documents, and what to expect at the border. Peace of mind before you fly.

🗺️

Tourist Traps Everywhere

Google "best Chongqing restaurants" and you'll find overpriced tourist spots. Real local gems? Impossible to find alone.

I take you where locals actually eat and hang out. The hotpot place with zero English reviews. The viewpoint with no selfie sticks.

True Stories

WHAT HAPPENS WITHOUT A LOCAL FIXER

These aren't hypotheticals. They happen to foreigners in Chongqing every single day.

⚠️ The Taxi That Cost $80 for a $5 Ride

A solo traveler landed at Chongqing Jiangbei Airport. No Chinese, no WeChat. Taxi driver saw a foreigner, turned off the meter, and demanded 500 RMB for a 15-minute ride that should cost 35 RMB. The traveler paid—he had no way to argue and no alternative.

⚠️ The Hotpot That Sent Someone to the Hospital

Two backpackers walked into a random hotpot restaurant. The menu was Chinese-only. They pointed at pictures and got served the spiciest broth in Chongqing. One ended up with severe stomach issues, cutting their trip short by 3 days.

⚠️ Stranded at Midnight with No Signal

A content creator explored the city alone. Her VPN stopped working. Google Maps went blank. She couldn't call a Didi without a Chinese number. Wandering for 2 hours in an unfamiliar district at midnight was not the content she planned to create.

⚠️ The Hotel That Didn't Accept Foreigners

Booked on Booking.com. Confirmation in hand. Arrived at 10 PM to hear "We don't accept foreigners." Why? The hotel wasn't licensed for international guests. Four hotels later, he finally found a room at double the price and half the quality.

⚠️ The "Attraction" That Was Actually a Shopping Trap

A family followed a "free tour" recommendation from their hotel. The "tea ceremony" turned into a high-pressure sales pitch. They spent $200 on tea they didn't want, because they didn't know how to say no in Chinese.

⚠️ The Metro Meltdown

A solo traveler tried taking Chongqing's metro. All signs in Chinese. Transferred to the wrong line three times. What should have been a 20-minute trip took 2 hours. She missed her entire morning itinerary and was too exhausted to enjoy the afternoon.
